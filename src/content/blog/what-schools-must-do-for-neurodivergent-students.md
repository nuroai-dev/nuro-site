## Start here

Most guides on this subject are written for parents. This one is written for the people who carry the duty.

If you lead a school or a huvudman organisation, the obligation to support students with ADHD, autism or dyslexia is not a matter of ambition or resources in the first instance. It is written into skollagen, it applies now, and it does not wait for a diagnosis. The uncomfortable part is that the gap between the duty and everyday practice is well documented, and [it is the school, not the student, that carries the legal risk](/blog/school-leaders-support-compliance-risk).

This page is the map. Each step links to a fuller explainer.


If you are a parent rather than a duty-holder, the same territory from your side is here: [your child's right to support in Swedish school](/blog/your-childs-right-to-support-in-swedish-school).
## The ladder is an obligation, not a menu

The support system is a sequence, and the earlier steps are duties in their own right rather than hoops to clear before the real support starts.

It begins with [ledning och stimulans](/blog/what-is-ledning-och-stimulans), the guidance every student is entitled to inside ordinary teaching. When that is not enough, [extra anpassningar](/blog/what-do-extra-anpassningar-look-like) apply within ordinary teaching, with no formal decision needed. When those are insufficient, the school must investigate, and särskilt stöd follows a formal decision by the rektor. [The distinction between these steps](/blog/what-skollagen-requires-extra-anpassningar-sarskilt-stod) is where most disputes begin.

Two points leaders should hold firmly. [The utredning is triggered by suspicion, not certainty](/blog/the-utredning-behind-sarskilt-stod), so waiting for proof is itself a deviation. And [the right does not depend on a diagnosis](/blog/support-without-a-diagnosis-school), so a waiting list for assessment is not a lawful reason to postpone support.

When särskilt stöd is decided, [the åtgärdsprogram has required contents](/blog/what-an-atgardsprogram-must-contain): the needs, the measures, who is responsible, and how and when it will be followed up. A document without follow-up is the most common way a compliant decision becomes a non-compliant reality.

## The duties that sit outside the ladder

Several obligations are easy to miss because they are not part of the stöd process at all.

The learning environment itself must be [accessible](/blog/what-is-an-accessible-learning-environment), which is a design duty rather than an individual accommodation. Absence is not a family matter to monitor passively, the school [has a duty to investigate it](/blog/school-duty-to-investigate-absence). Learning tools a student needs are [to be provided without charge](/blog/digital-learning-tools-schools-must-provide-free). Support [follows the student when they change schools](/blog/support-follows-student-changing-schools), which makes överlämning a compliance step and not a courtesy.

There are also duties with their own legal machinery. Schools must act on [kränkande behandling](/blog/krankande-behandling-school-duty-to-act), and a failure to provide support can itself amount to [discrimination through bristande tillgänglighet](/blog/failing-to-support-npf-student-can-be-discrimination). Behaviour that reads as defiance is very often [a signal about an environment that is not working](/blog/trygghet-och-studiero-when-behaviour-is-a-signal), which matters because the response chosen determines whether the situation improves or escalates.

## Where inspections keep finding the gaps

The recurring findings are consistent enough to use as a self-check.

Support arrives late, and [the early support guarantee has not been delivering as intended](/blog/swedens-early-support-guarantee-not-delivering). The [national inspection picture shows a persistent support gap](/blog/swedens-school-inspectorate-2025-support-gap), and a dedicated review of learning environments for students with NPF [found the same weaknesses repeating](/blog/skolinspektionen-npf-learning-environment-review). Support is also distributed unevenly: [girls with NPF receive less särskilt stöd](/blog/girls-with-npf-receive-less-sarskilt-stod), and [ADHD diagnoses have been rising fastest among girls](/blog/adhd-diagnoses-rising-fastest-among-girls), which means the historical pattern of who gets noticed is not a safe guide to who needs support.

The highest-stakes failure mode is absence. It [starts earlier and more quietly than most schools assume](/blog/school-absence-starts-earlier), Sweden [does not even collect it as national statistics](/blog/sweden-doesnt-count-school-absence), and the [cost of letting it harden](/blog/cost-of-school-absence-false-economy) falls on the student first and the municipality later.

## The constraints, taken seriously

None of the above is news to a rektor. The reason it persists is usually structural, and pretending otherwise helps nobody.

[Principals report that they do not receive sufficient financial resources](/blog/principals-say-they-lack-resources-for-npf) for their support duties. The [elevhälsa is thinly staffed](/blog/elevhalsa-understaffed-support-frontline) for the frontline role it is given. There is [a national shortage of the two special education roles](/blog/two-special-education-roles-one-shortage), and [Sweden needs more of them](/blog/sweden-needs-more-special-education-teachers) than it is producing. Teachers [are not consistently prepared](/blog/are-teachers-prepared-for-neurodivergent-students) for the range of needs in an ordinary classroom.

Two levers are worth knowing precisely. [Tilläggsbelopp](/blog/what-is-tillaggsbelopp-school-support-funding) exists for extraordinary measures for a named student, and it is not what funds the everyday adjustments most neurodivergent students need, which come out of ordinary funding. And [documentation burden](/blog/teacher-documentation-burden-and-npf-support) is the constraint leaders most often mistake for a motivation problem: when recording support competes with delivering it, delivery loses.

## Assessment, grading and the exceptions

Two rules decide whether adapted teaching translates into fair outcomes.

[Undantagsbestämmelsen](/blog/undantagsbestammelsen-grading-neurodivergent-students) allows a teacher to disregard certain requirements when a disability prevents a student from meeting them, and it is applied unevenly because it is poorly understood. [National tests can be adapted](/blog/adapting-national-tests-for-neurodivergent-students) within stated limits. Getting both right is what keeps a student's support from quietly turning into a lower grade.

## Bringing AI in without breaking the rules

If AI is on your agenda, the conditions are already written down. [Skolverket's guidance](/blog/what-skolverket-says-about-ai-in-school) says the huvudman must ensure use complies with the EU AI Act, that personal data and student texts should not be handed over, and that AI may not replace a teacher's professional judgement or grading decisions. Special education [has its own perspective on where AI helps](/blog/spsm-special-education-perspective-on-ai), and the [national policy debate](/blog/sweden-ai-in-schools-npf-support) has so far focused on students writing with AI rather than on using it to deliver support the law already requires.

Also worth tracking: [the support provisions themselves are proposed to be rewritten](/blog/sweden-proposes-rewriting-school-support-law), so systems built around today's exact terminology should be built to survive a change in it.

## Where Nuro fits

Nuro is built for the part of this that is chronically under-resourced: doing the adaptation and producing the record.

It adapts material so a student can reach the same goals rather than lower ones, and it turns what actually happens into documentation the school can use in an utredning, an åtgärdsprogram, or a follow-up. The professional judgement and every formal decision stay with the teacher and the rektor, which is both the legal requirement and the only version worth having.

The reason we build it is on this page. The duty is not in doubt, the constraints are real, and the gap between them is where students are lost.
