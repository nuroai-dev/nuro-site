## The short answer

Yes, support applies in fritidshem. Fritidshem is part of the student's education, not a separate service that runs after the education ends, so the right to [extra anpassningar and särskilt stöd](/blog/what-skollagen-requires-extra-anpassningar-sarskilt-stod) covers the afternoon as well as the lesson.

There is also a second, less known right. A student who needs särskilt stöd in their development can be entitled to fritidshem itself, regardless of whether the family meets the usual conditions.

Both rights are real. In practice, the afternoon is where support most often quietly falls away, and Sweden's school inspectorate has documented exactly that.

## What the law says

Skolverket sets out the basic duty plainly: "Varje kommun ska erbjuda utbildning i fritidshem för elever i kommunens förskoleklass, grundskola och anpassade grundskola."

Then comes the provision that many families never hear about: "En elev ska även i andra fall erbjudas utbildning i fritidshem, om eleven av fysiska, psykiska eller andra skäl behöver särskilt stöd i sin utveckling i form av sådan utbildning."

Read that carefully. Fritidshem is normally offered on the basis of the parents' work or studies. This provision is a separate route. If a child needs fritidshem itself as support for their development, for physical, psychological or other reasons, the school is to offer it anyway. A parent working part time, studying, or at home is not automatically a reason to refuse.

Two other practical points from the same guidance: fritidshem is offered during the part of the day the student is not in school and during school holidays, and it is offered up to and including the spring term of the year the student turns 13.

## Why the afternoon is where it breaks

The needs of a neurodivergent student do not end at two o'clock, but the scaffolding often does.

The morning has structure: a timetable, a known room, a teacher who knows the plan. The afternoon has more free time, more noise, more unstructured social negotiation, more transitions, and often different adults. For a student who relies on [predictability and clear framing](/blog/what-is-tydliggorande-pedagogik), on [visual support](/blog/what-is-bildstod-akk-visual-support), or on [a low-arousal response when things get difficult](/blog/what-is-lagaffektivt-bemotande), that combination is the hardest part of the day, not the easiest.

This is also where early warning signs surface. A child who holds it together through lessons and falls apart at fritids is not a child with an afternoon problem. That is information about the cost of the whole day.

## What Skolinspektionen found

In 2024 the school inspectorate published a thematic quality review with a title that names the issue directly: "Elevens hela dag som utgångspunkt för stöd", the student's whole day as the starting point for support. It examined work with extra anpassningar and särskilt stöd across school and fritidshem, focusing on the youngest students in förskoleklass and lågstadiet.

Of the 30 schools reviewed, 19 were assessed as needing to strengthen their work in the areas examined.

An important caveat, in the inspectorate's own words: "Iakttagelserna och slutsatserna gäller de 30 verksamheter som har granskats och avser inte att ge en nationell bild av förhållandena." This is a qualitative review of 30 schools, not national statistics, and it should not be quoted as though it were.

Within those 30 schools, the pattern is specific. The report states that it is particularly fritidshem that ends up in the shade of the support work, and that staff there do not have the same conditions to take part in it. Structured opportunities for fritidshem staff to share what works and what does not are sometimes missing. Where collaboration does happen, it often starts from the student's needs in school, and support in fritidshem is rarely discussed.

The same gap appears with elevhälsa. The report notes that at several schools the student health team's supporting role is clearly focused on classroom teaching, and fritidshem falls into the shade. In some cases specialpedagogisk competence had no clear mandate from the rektor to work with fritidshem staff or with elevassistenter at all, leaving those groups to seek it out themselves.

The responsibility is named, and it is organisational. The rektor is responsible for creating the conditions for staff in school and fritidshem to work together, and for making clear who is responsible for what.

The encouraging half of the finding: a third of the reviewed schools were assessed as working systematically from the student's need for support across the whole day, which shows it is possible. What set them apart was that they started from the assumption that a student's needs can look different in school and in fritidshem, and that the rektor had clarified roles and created the conditions to collaborate.

## What a parent can ask

- Does my child's [åtgärdsprogram](/blog/what-an-atgardsprogram-must-contain) or set of extra anpassningar describe the afternoon, or only lessons?
- Who at fritidshem knows the plan, and how did they find out?
- Are fritidshem staff present when support for my child is planned and followed up?
- If my child needs fritidshem itself as support for their development, has the school considered that route?

If the answer to the first three is vague, that is worth raising. Support that exists only on paper for the morning is not support for the whole day.
