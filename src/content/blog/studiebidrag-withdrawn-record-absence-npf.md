## The short answer

On 8 July 2026, CSN reported that "43 600 gymnasieelever, eller 11,8 procent, sitt studiebidrag indraget" for unexcused absence during the 2025/26 school year. That is "4 500 fler än föregående läsår, då andelen var 10,8 procent", and it is the highest figure recorded.

The threshold is lower than most families expect. "Om du har upprepad ogiltig frånvaro på sammanlagt fyra timmar eller mer under en månad, kan skolan rapportera det till CSN." Four hours across a month, which for a student who cannot get through the door on bad mornings is not a lot of mornings.

And the consequence does not stop at the student. "Om du har ogiltig frånvaro stoppar vi alla dina bidrag från CSN. Men även andra bidrag påverkas, till exempel flerbarnstillägg, underhållsstöd och bostadsbidrag från Försäkringskassan."

## Why this matters differently for a neurodivergent student

For many students, absence is a choice. For a student with ADHD, autism or dyslexia whose support is not working, absence is usually the last step in a sequence: the demands exceed what the adaptations cover, the school day becomes unmanageable, and staying away becomes the only available regulation strategy.

That sequence is the one Swedish schools already have a duty to interrupt. We have written about [the school's obligation to investigate absence](/blog/school-duty-to-investigate-absence) and about how [support at gymnasiet is governed by the same law as earlier years](/blog/npf-support-rights-gymnasium). The duty does not soften because the student is sixteen.

So the uncomfortable structure is this. The same school that owes a student the support is also the body that decides whether the resulting absence is reported as unexcused. When support is missing, the failure can reach the family as a reduction in household income.

## The discretion most families do not know exists

This is the part worth reading twice, because it is written plainly by CSN and rarely mentioned in the letter that lands at home.

"Det är skolan som bedömer vad som räknas som ogiltig frånvaro."

And: "Skolan kan i vissa fall välja att inte rapportera frånvaro till CSN. Det kan de göra om frånvaron till exempel beror på sjukdom, mobbning eller andra personliga skäl som skolan känner till."

Note the final clause. The discretion rests on what the school knows. A school that has never been told why a student is absent has nothing to exercise judgement about, and the absence travels to CSN as a bare number of hours.

That is not an invitation to relabel absence. It is the opposite. It means the reason belongs in writing, in the school's own records, at the time it happens rather than after the grant is withdrawn.

## What the rise appears to be made of

CSN is careful about the cause, and so should we be. According to the schools themselves, "uppgången beror främst på skärpta rutiner för inrapportering till CSN. Fyra av tio skolor uppger att de rapporterat in fler elever än tidigare. Men många skolor uppger också att de ser en trend där allt fler elever har hög ogiltig frånvaro."

In other words, part of the record is better reporting rather than more absence. That distinction matters, and it cuts both ways: tightened routines mean a student whose absence would have passed unnoticed two years ago is now more likely to be reported for the same behaviour.

CSN's Peter Engberg notes where it concentrates: "Vi ser en generell uppgång där indraget studiebidrag ökar bland såväl pojkar som flickor och i samtliga årskurser, i synnerhet i årskurs tre."

Year three is the end of gymnasiet. It is the point at which a student who has been quietly under-supported for two years runs out of room, which is precisely the pattern that [Sweden's failure to count absence consistently](/blog/sweden-doesnt-count-school-absence) makes hard to catch early.

## What to do, in order

1. **Put the reason in writing, early.** Not after the withdrawal. If absence connects to unmet support, an unmanageable schedule or health, the school needs it documented while it is happening, because its discretion depends on what it knows.
2. **Ask for the absence to trigger the duty it is supposed to trigger.** Repeated absence is a signal the school must investigate. Ask, in writing, what is being done and whether extra anpassningar or särskilt stöd are being considered.
3. **Check the record before you accept the number.** "Om du anser att skolans uppgifter om din ogiltiga frånvaro inte stämmer, ska du i första hand kontakta din skola." Registered absence is frequently wrong in ordinary ways: a lesson attended late, an agreed adaptation recorded as skipping.
4. **Appeal if the decision is wrong.** "Om du anser att CSN:s beslut är fel kan du överklaga beslutet."
5. **Expect the knock-on effects and plan for them.** Flerbarnstillägg, underhållsstöd and bostadsbidrag can all follow. A family that knows this is coming can act sooner than one that discovers it in June.

## The part that should trouble a school leader

A student who reaches four hours of unexcused absence in a month has usually been struggling for longer than a month. By the time the report reaches CSN, the school has had many chances to notice.

That is the real cost of a system that spots absence at the point it becomes financially reportable rather than at the point it becomes educationally obvious. We have written before about [why absence is a false economy](/blog/cost-of-school-absence-false-economy). This is the same argument with a bill attached to it.

## Where Nuro fits

Nuro exists to make the earlier signal visible. Absence is a late indicator. What comes first is a student who stops starting tasks, hands less in, and gets quieter, and [the starting problem](/blog/executive-function-why-your-child-cannot-start) usually shows up weeks before anyone is counting hours.

If a school can see that drift while it is still drift, the conversation is about adjusting support. If it cannot, the first formal record of the problem may well be a letter from CSN.
