## The short answer

From 1 August 2026, phones are collected at the start of the school day and handed back at the end. Skolverket states the rule plainly: "Från den 1 augusti 2026 ska skolan vara mobilfri i de obligatoriska skolformerna, fritidshemmet och öppen fritidsverksamhet. Det innebär att elevernas mobiltelefoner ska samlas in vid skoldagens början och lämnas tillbaka vid skoldagens slut."

If your child uses their phone as a learning tool, the rule is not the end of that. Skolverket is explicit that exceptions exist: "Det finns möjlighet att göra undantag, till exempel om en mobiltelefon behövs som stöd för en elev."

But the more useful detail is in how the rule is built, and it is easy to miss.

## Assistive use is inside the rule, not an exception to it

Read the operative sentence carefully:

"Om det finns särskilda skäl får rektorn eller en lärare tillåta en enskild elev att använda en mobiltelefon eller annan elektronisk kommunikationsutrustning även i andra fall än inom ramen för lärarens instruktioner, extra anpassningar eller särskilt stöd."

The words that matter are "även i andra fall än". Särskilda skäl are for cases *other than* three situations that are already permitted: use on the teacher's instructions, use as part of [extra anpassningar](/blog/what-do-extra-anpassningar-look-like), and use as part of [särskilt stöd](/blog/what-skollagen-requires-extra-anpassningar-sarskilt-stod).

So a phone that is genuinely part of a student's support is not asking for an exemption from the rule. It is already inside the frame the rule describes. The "särskilda skäl" route, which Skolverket says "ska dock tillämpas restriktivt och innebär inte att undantag kan göras för hela undervisningsgrupper", is the narrower path for everything else.

That distinction changes what you should do about it, and when.

## Why this lands hard on neurodivergent students

For a lot of students the phone is a social object. For some neurodivergent students it is closer to a prosthesis for executive function:

- Text-to-speech for a student with [dyslexia](/blog/dyslexia-in-the-classroom-what-helps), which is often the difference between reading the assignment and not.
- Timers and visual countdowns that make an abstract "twenty minutes" concrete.
- Reminders and checklists carrying the parts of [tydliggörande pedagogik](/blog/what-is-tydliggorande-pedagogik) that a student cannot hold in working memory.
- [Bildstöd and AKK apps](/blog/what-is-bildstod-akk-visual-support) for a student whose communication does not run best through speech.
- Medical monitoring, which is a separate question from learning support but ends up in the same basket at 08:15.

Removing that on the first morning of term, from a student who has spent a year building routines around it, is not a neutral act. It is the removal of an adaptation, even when nobody intended it that way.

It is worth saying the other half honestly: for many neurodivergent students the phone is also the single biggest source of distraction in the room, and a calmer classroom is a real gain for exactly the students who struggle most with [trygghet och studiero](/blog/trygghet-och-studiero-when-behaviour-is-a-signal). The point is not that the rule is wrong. The point is that the student who needs the tool and the student who needs the quiet are frequently the same student, and only documentation tells the school which is which.

## What to do before the term starts

The mechanism the law gives you is documentation, and August is the month to use it.

1. **Name the function, not the device.** "He needs his phone" invites a no. "She uses text-to-speech to access texts at grade level, and it lives on the phone" describes an adaptation. The school can then decide whether the phone or a school device delivers it.
2. **Get it written into the extra anpassningar,** or into the [åtgärdsprogram](/blog/what-an-atgardsprogram-must-contain) if the student has särskilt stöd. That is what moves the use from a favour into the frame the rule already recognises.
3. **Ask what the school will provide instead.** Schools must supply the [digital tools that teaching requires](/blog/digital-learning-tools-schools-must-provide-free) without charging families. If a school laptop covers text-to-speech, that solves it just as well and is more robust than a phone in a pocket.
4. **Settle it before the first morning,** not at the collection point with a queue behind you. A decision made in advance is a decision made calmly, by the people who know the student.
5. **Ask the student.** They know exactly which functions they lean on, and they have [a legal right to be heard](/blog/does-a-student-get-a-say-in-their-own-support) about their own support.

Note the scope: the rule as Skolverket describes it covers the compulsory school forms, fritidshem and öppen fritidsverksamhet. It does not describe gymnasiet, where [the support rules still apply](/blog/npf-support-rights-gymnasium) but this particular collection requirement does not.

## Where Nuro fits

The pattern here is the one that runs through most support failures in Swedish schools. The right exists, the accommodation is real, and it fails in practice because it lives in one teacher's memory rather than in writing.

Nuro's job is to make that documentation a by-product of everyday teaching rather than an extra task somebody has to remember in August. When the adaptation a student actually uses is recorded as it happens, the answer at the phone basket is not a negotiation. It is a lookup.
