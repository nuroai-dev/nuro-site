## The short answer

Yes, and it is written into Skollagen twice.

The first is general. Skollagen 1 kap. 10 § says the child's own view has to be sought: "Barnets inställning ska så långt det är möjligt klarläggas. Barn ska ha möjlighet att fritt uttrycka sina åsikter i alla frågor som rör honom eller henne." The child's view shall as far as possible be established, and children shall have the opportunity to express their views freely in all matters that concern them.

The second is specific to support. When a school writes an [åtgärdsprogram](/blog/what-an-atgardsprogram-must-contain), Skollagen 3 kap. 9 § requires that "Eleven och elevens vårdnadshavare ska ges möjlighet att delta när ett åtgärdsprogram utarbetas." The student, not only the guardian, must be given the opportunity to take part.

Sweden went further in 2020, when the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child became Swedish law through lag (2018:1197). The child's right to be heard in matters affecting them is not a principle the school may weigh against convenience. It is law.

## What the right is not

It is not a veto. Skolverket is direct about the limit: the student and guardian take part, but "De kan inte kräva en specifik stödinsats." They cannot demand a specific support measure. The school decides what the support will be, based on the [utredning](/blog/the-utredning-behind-sarskilt-stod) of the student's needs.

That distinction is worth understanding before a meeting rather than during one. The student's influence is over what the problem actually is, which is the part the school cannot see from the outside. The choice of measure sits with the professionals, and if the family disagrees with the decision, the route is [an appeal against the åtgärdsprogram](/blog/how-to-appeal-a-sarskilt-stod-decision), not a longer argument in the room.

## Being asked is not the same as being heard

This is where the right tends to evaporate, and it is worth being honest that this part is our reading rather than a rule in the statute.

A school can satisfy the letter of 3 kap. 9 § by inviting a fourteen year old into a room with three adults, asking "how do you think it is going?", and writing down the answer. The student says it is fine. The record now shows the student took part.

For a neurodivergent student, that format is close to designed to fail:

- A student who processes spoken language slowly is being asked to compose an answer at conversational speed, in front of adults, about their hardest subject.
- A student who masks all day will produce the answer that ends the conversation fastest, because that is the skill they have spent years building.
- A student with a demand-avoidant profile hears a question about school as a demand about school, and the honest answer costs more than the polite one.
- A student is being asked to summarise a whole term from memory, when the difficulty is precisely that some days went well and some did not and nobody wrote down which.

None of that means the student has nothing to say. It means the method collected nothing.

## What actually collects the student's view

The practical fixes are the same ones the [accessible learning environment](/blog/what-is-an-accessible-learning-environment) is built on, applied to the meeting itself:

- Send the questions in advance, in writing. Preparation time is not an advantage, it is the difference between an answer and a silence.
- Let the answer arrive in another channel. Written, drawn, recorded, through a trusted adult, or with [bildstöd and AKK](/blog/what-is-bildstod-akk-visual-support) for a student who does not answer best in speech.
- Ask about specific situations, not the whole term. "What happened in maths on Tuesday" is answerable. "How is school going" is not.
- Ask what helps as well as what is hard. Students are rarely asked what already works for them, and they usually know.
- Separate the student's account from the assessment of it. The point of the first is to find out what school feels like from the inside.

The same principle runs through [tydliggörande pedagogik](/blog/what-is-tydliggorande-pedagogik) and through a well-run [utvecklingssamtal](/blog/what-is-an-utvecklingssamtal): make the expectation visible in advance, and let the student meet it in a form that suits how they think.

## Why the student's account is not a courtesy

The student is the only person present in every single lesson. Teachers see one subject, guardians see the aftermath at home, elevhälsan sees the student when something has already gone wrong. The student is the only continuous observer of their own school day, which makes them the primary source on the thing the school is trying to establish.

That is also why an unheard student is an expensive one. When the account is missing, the support gets designed around the visible symptom rather than the cause, and support aimed at the wrong thing is how a student ends up [gradually stopping coming](/blog/school-absence-starts-earlier).

## Where Nuro fits

Nuro is built on the assumption that the student's own signal matters and that one meeting a term is a poor way to collect it. A student who tells us, in the moment, that a task was too long or the instructions were unclear is producing exactly the evidence 1 kap. 10 § asks the school to seek, in the form that student can actually produce it, on the day it happened rather than reconstructed months later in a room full of adults.

That does not replace the meeting or the school's decision. It means that when the meeting happens, the student's view is already documented, specific and impossible to summarise away.
