## The short answer

Nothing about your child's support expires in June. Skolverket is clear about when särskilt stöd ends: the rektor decides to end an åtgärdsprogram "när det bedöms att eleven inte längre är i behov av särskilt stöd". The end of the school year is not that assessment. It is a holiday.

What does change in August is the people. New teachers, sometimes a new mentor, occasionally a new stage of school entirely. And the uncomfortable truth families discover every autumn is that a documented adaptation and a remembered one look identical in June, and completely different in September.

So the first weeks of term are not a settling-in period. They are the window in which last year's support either transfers or silently disappears.

## What carries over, whether or not anyone mentions it

Three things are worth knowing before you have the conversation.

**The trigger is a risk, not a diagnosis, and the response must be prompt.** The rule reads: "Om det kan befaras att en elev inte kommer att uppfylla de betygskriterier eller kriterier för bedömning av kunskaper som minst ska uppfyllas ska eleven skyndsamt ges stöd i form av extra anpassningar." Skyndsamt means promptly. A school that plans to "see how the first term goes" before acting on a known difficulty has the timing backwards.

**A diagnosis is never a condition.** "En diagnos får aldrig vara ett villkor för att en elev ska få stöd i skolan." This matters in August because a new teacher who does not know your child may reach for the assessment as the reason to wait. It is not a valid reason.

**An åtgärdsprogram has a built-in review, and that review is a date, not a season.** "Av åtgärdsprogrammet ska det framgå när åtgärderna ska följas upp och utvärderas och vem som är ansvarig." If your child has one, it should name both. Ask which named person now holds it, because that person may have changed while the document did not.

## The handover is stronger on paper than in practice

When a student changes school, there is a duty to pass on what the new school needs. We wrote about that in [support follows the student when they change schools](/blog/support-follows-student-changing-schools).

The more common event, moving up a year inside the same school, is governed more by local routine than by anything as firm. Skolverket's guidance on transitions asks that documentation carry "aktuell och relevant information kopplat till progressionen i barnens och elevernas utveckling, lärande och det undervisningsinnehåll de tagit del av", and that it avoid "värdeladdade ord eller uppgifter om personliga egenskaper". For students who need support, it adds that "är det betydelsefullt att den avlämnande skolan pratar med vårdnadshavaren och eleven".

Read that as what it is: good practice, dependent on someone doing it in the last busy week of June. This is precisely why an adaptation that only ever lived in a conversation is the one that does not survive the summer.

## This August has an extra reset in it

There is a specific reason to be attentive this particular term. On 1 August 2026, ordningsregler become [skolregler, which must contain a plan for consequences](/blog/skolregler-consequence-plan-neurodivergent-students), a förväntansdokument becomes mandatory, and schools go [mobile-free, with phones collected at the start of the day](/blog/mobile-free-school-2026-assistive-phone-use).

Every school is therefore writing new routines this month, in the same weeks it is also rebuilding its classes. If your child's support touches any of that, and a phone used for text-to-speech or timers certainly does, this is the moment to get it written into the new routine rather than argued about at the collection point in week three.

## What to check, and when

1. **Ask who now holds the plan.** One question, by email, in week one: which teacher is responsible for my child's extra anpassningar this year, and have they seen last year's? It is a neutral question that surfaces a gap immediately.
2. **Ask for it in writing if it is not already.** Adaptations that exist only as goodwill do not transfer. Get them into the [extra anpassningar](/blog/what-do-extra-anpassningar-look-like) or, where there is särskilt stöd, the [åtgärdsprogram](/blog/what-an-atgardsprogram-must-contain).
3. **Name the function, not the child.** "She needs help getting started and a written order of steps" travels between teachers. "She struggles" does not. If the difficulty is [the starting problem](/blog/executive-function-why-your-child-cannot-start), say so in those words.
4. **Check the new school rules for your child's exception.** If assistive use of a device is part of the support, ask how the new skolregler handle it, and get the answer before the routine sets.
5. **Do not wait for the utvecklingssamtal.** It usually falls after the point where a term has already gone wrong. It is still the formal venue, and we have a [guide to preparing for it](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-your-childs-utvecklingssamtal), but week one is an email, not a meeting.
6. **Watch the second and third weeks, not the first.** Most children hold together while everything is new. The signal to watch for is the drop that comes when novelty wears off and the actual demands land.

## Why the first weeks matter more than they should

A term rarely fails in November. It fails in the first month, quietly, while a student who was managing last year works out that the things that made it possible are gone and that asking for them again means explaining themselves to someone new. Many will not ask. They will absorb it, fall behind by degrees, and arrive at the autumn meeting already behind, at which point the conversation becomes about catching up rather than about what was removed.

That is also the point at which absence often starts, which is the whole reason this window is worth the two emails it costs.

## Where Nuro fits

None of this should be a parent's job. The reason it becomes one is that the record of what actually helps a student tends to live in the person who worked it out, rather than in the school's system, so every August it has to be rebuilt from memory by someone who was not there.

Nuro exists to close that gap. When the adaptations a student actually uses are captured as teaching happens, a new teacher in August inherits something real instead of a name on a class list, and the student does not have to spend the first month proving they needed help in the first place.
