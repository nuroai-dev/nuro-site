## The short answer

Handing work in is not administrative tidiness. It is the mechanism by which knowledge becomes assessable. Work that exists in a notebook, a drive folder or a half-finished document has, for grading purposes, not happened.

Swedish law has exactly one route to no grade being set, and it is not this one. Skollagen 15 kap. 27 § says: "Om det saknas underlag för bedömning av en elevs kunskaper på grund av elevens frånvaro, ska betyg inte sättas."

Read the condition carefully. It is "på grund av elevens frånvaro", because of the student's absence. A student who is in the room every day and simply cannot get work delivered does not meet it. The teacher has attendance, has seen work in class, has something to judge. So a grade is set, on what the teacher actually has.

That is the whole problem in one sentence. Absence produces no grade. Non-delivery produces a bad one.

## The difference between a streck and an F

Both are non-passing, and neither is a good outcome, but they say different things.

An F means the teacher had material to assess and judged that it did not reach the criteria. A streck, the dash entered when 15 kap. 27 § applies, means there was nothing to assess because the student was not there.

For a family, the practical consequence of the distinction is this: if your child is present and working, nobody is going to arrive at a streck. Whatever else happens, the course will be graded. So the delivery gap is not a neutral administrative problem that resolves itself at the end of term. It converts, quietly and on schedule, into a grade that says the student did not know the material.

Which, for the student we are describing, is not true. That is what makes this particular failure so corrosive.

## Why this lands on neurodivergent students

If the work is done, knowledge is not the obstacle. What has failed is the step between finishing something and releasing it: locating the right file, deciding it is good enough, opening the right portal, doing it before a deadline that felt distant until it was yesterday.

Those are executive functions, and they are the exact functions affected by ADHD and often by autism. We have written about [why a student can be unable to start](/blog/executive-function-why-your-child-cannot-start). Finishing has the same structure. A task can be complete and still not be over, because the ending has its own initiation cost.

Teachers see the output, not the process. A student who hands in nothing looks identical to a student who did nothing, and the grade cannot tell them apart.

## This is a support question, not a discipline question

Here is the part schools frequently get backwards. An inability to deliver work is a need. It sits inside the same duty as any other, which is [triggered by a student risking not meeting the criteria](/blog/what-skollagen-requires-extra-anpassningar-sarskilt-stod), and support at gymnasiet is governed by [the same law as earlier school years](/blog/npf-support-rights-gymnasium).

So the adaptations should target the actual failure point, not the knowledge that is already there. In practice that means things like:

- Splitting one deadline into two or three checkpoints, so the gap between "done" and "delivered" is never long enough to swallow the work.
- Submitting in class, in front of the teacher, rather than at home at 23:55.
- Allowing an alternative delivery format, including showing the work orally, when the barrier is the writing-up rather than the knowing.
- A named person who checks whether it landed, because a student who forgets to submit will equally forget to check that they submitted.

None of this is exotic. It is ordinary [extra anpassningar](/blog/what-do-extra-anpassningar-look-like) pointed at delivery instead of comprehension. A school that has documented adaptations for reading and none for submission has usually not asked why the work is missing.

Worth noting separately: [undantagsbestämmelsen](/blog/undantagsbestammelsen-grading-neurodivergent-students) lets a teacher disregard single grading criteria when a disability is a direct barrier. That rule is about criteria, not deadlines, so it is not a fix for this. It is worth knowing about, and worth not confusing with this.

## If the grade has already gone

Two things are true at once. First, a set grade stands; it is a professional judgement, not an administrative decision that can be appealed away. Second, the door is not closed. Skollagen 15 kap. 28 § is short: "Den som vill ha betyg från gymnasieskolan har rätt att genomgå prövning."

A prövning is, in Skolverket's words, "en bedömning av en persons kunskaper i förhållande till de betygskriterier som finns i kursplaner eller ämnesplaner". For a student whose problem was delivery rather than knowledge, that is a route that assesses the thing that was actually true.

It is a recovery mechanism, not a plan. Using it every term means the adaptations upstream are still missing.

## What to ask for, in writing

1. Name the pattern rather than the incident. Not "he missed the biology assignment", but "work is regularly completed and not submitted, across subjects".
2. Ask for it to be treated as a support need, and ask which adaptations will target delivery specifically.
3. Ask who checks that submissions land, and when.
4. Ask for the deadline structure to change rather than the deadline itself to move. A later single deadline usually reproduces the same failure with more anxiety attached.

If attendance starts slipping too, the stakes widen quickly, because [absence at gymnasiet now carries a financial consequence](/blog/studiebidrag-withdrawn-record-absence-npf) as well as an academic one.

## Where Nuro fits

The signal here is visible long before the grade is. A student who is completing work and not delivering it produces a very particular pattern: activity without submissions, effort without evidence.

That pattern is exactly what a system watching the work itself can see, and what a system watching only the gradebook cannot see until the term is over and the grade is set. Nuro exists to make that difference in timing, so the conversation happens while the work still counts.
