## The short answer

If your child can tell you precisely what the assignment is, when it is due and why it matters, and then sits in front of it for forty minutes without writing a word, you are not watching a motivation problem. You are watching the gap between two different abilities.

SPSM, Sweden's national agency for special needs education, defines the second one as part of a person's executive functions: "Exekutiva funktioner är ett samlingsbegrepp för flera olika kognitiva funktioner som hjälper oss att styra och reglera våra tankar, känslor och beteenden." The list it gives includes "självreglering, olika uppmärksamhetsfunktioner, arbetsminne, impulskontroll, flexibilitet, förmågan att komma igång och fullfölja uppgifter, planering och organisering."

Read that list again. **Getting started and finishing a task is itself a named function.** It can be impaired while comprehension, intelligence and willingness are all perfectly intact. That is not a metaphor or a kind way of putting it. It is how the agency that advises Swedish schools describes it.

## The word that does the damage

Parents almost always arrive at this having heard one word, from a teacher, a relative, or their own worst three in the morning: lazy.

SPSM addresses that misreading directly: "Ibland kan omgivningen misstolka en låg aktivitetsnivå och tro att eleven är lat, passiv eller ointresserad."

That sentence is worth carrying into every meeting you have about your child. The misinterpretation is documented, it is expected, and it is named as a mistake by the national authority. When someone reaches for the word lazy, they are describing what the difficulty looks like from outside, not what it is.

## What it looks like from the inside

The pattern parents describe is remarkably consistent, and it maps onto specific functions rather than onto attitude.

- **The starting problem.** SPSM notes that with ADHD, "många har en mer långsam arbetstakt, svårare för att ta initiativ, komma igång och behöver ofta mer tid". The task is understood. The first move is the wall.
- **Time that does not behave.** "Elever med adhd kan ha svag eller outvecklad tidsuppfattning." Twenty minutes and two hours feel similar from the inside, which is why a deadline in three weeks exerts no pull until the night before, when it exerts all of it at once.
- **The work that exists but never arrives.** Finished assignments that stay in the bag are a completion and organisation failure, not a comprehension one. The student did the hard part and lost the last, apparently trivial step.
- **The collapse at the kitchen table.** Executive load does not disappear at 15:00. A child who has spent the entire school day compensating has nothing left for the homework, which is why the same task can be impossible on Tuesday and fine on Saturday.

## This is not a favour to ask for

Here is the part that matters legally, and that most families are never told.

Support with planning, structure and getting started is not an indulgence granted to a struggling student. It is exactly the kind of thing [extra anpassningar](/blog/what-do-extra-anpassningar-look-like) exist to deliver, within regular teaching, without any diagnosis and without a formal decision. If those are not enough, the school has a duty to investigate whether the student needs [särskilt stöd](/blog/what-skollagen-requires-extra-anpassningar-sarskilt-stod).

SPSM is also specific about what helps, which is useful because it lets you ask for named things rather than for sympathy:

- "Du kan stötta eleven med visuella scheman, planeringar och arbetsordningar."
- "Det kan också vara till stor hjälp om du erbjuder eleven täta, korta avstämningar under arbetets gång."

Those two sentences describe a system, and it is worth seeing why it works. A visual plan moves the sequence out of the student's working memory and onto paper, where it stops competing for the same limited resource. Short, frequent check-ins do something subtler: they break one large, undifferentiated task, which is where an executive function difficulty gets stuck, into a series of small starts, which is the exact step the student struggles with. The support is aimed at the impaired function rather than at the missing output. That distinction is the whole of [tydliggörande pedagogik](/blog/what-is-tydliggorande-pedagogik).

## How to ask, so that it lands

1. **Describe the function, not the outcome.** "He is behind on three assignments" invites a conversation about effort. "He can tell me the whole assignment and cannot make the first move, so he needs help getting started and a written order of steps" describes a need the school knows how to meet.
2. **Ask for the named supports.** Visual schedules, written work orders, short frequent check-ins. Asking for specific, recognised measures is much harder to answer with a vague reassurance than asking for "more support".
3. **Get it written down.** Verbal goodwill leaves with the teacher who offered it. Ask for it in the extra anpassningar, or in the [åtgärdsprogram](/blog/what-an-atgardsprogram-must-contain) if there is särskilt stöd, so it survives a change of staff.
4. **Use the meeting you already have.** The [utvecklingssamtal](/blog/what-is-an-utvecklingssamtal) is the built-in venue, and it goes better with preparation. We wrote a [guide to preparing for it](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-your-childs-utvecklingssamtal).
5. **Do not assume gymnasiet is different.** Executive demands rise sharply there, with longer deadlines and less scaffolding, exactly as adult support falls away. The [duties still apply](/blog/npf-support-rights-gymnasium).

## Why this matters more than it sounds

The same barrier sits at the other end of a task, where [work gets finished and never handed in](/blog/work-done-never-handed-in-gymnasiet). A student who cannot start is not failing at homework. They are accumulating daily evidence that they are lazy, from adults whose opinion they care about, while working harder than anyone can see. That is the mechanism by which a capable child becomes a child who stops trying, and eventually a child who stops going. School avoidance rarely begins with disliking school. It often begins with a task that will not start, repeated until the student concludes the problem is them.

## Where Nuro fits

The supports SPSM lists are not expensive or exotic. A visual plan, a written order of steps, a check-in every ten minutes. They fail for one boring reason: they depend on one teacher, with thirty students, remembering to do them consistently for months.

Nuro is built for that gap. It breaks work into clear, structured steps automatically, so the getting-started support arrives every time rather than on the days someone has capacity, and so what the student actually needed is documented as it happens rather than reconstructed at the next meeting.
